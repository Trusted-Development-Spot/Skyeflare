How SkyeFlare Verifies Hosting Deals & Exposes Renewal Traps

Editorial Methodology · Updated Nov 22, 2026

Nearly every shared hosting provider uses dual-tier pricing: an aggressively discounted first-term rate designed to win signups, followed by an automated standard renewal rate that jumps between 150% and 350% once your initial term ends. Comparing only the headline monthly price leads buyers into unexpected Year 2 invoices.

01. Upfront Prepay vs. Monthly Flexibility

A "$2.49/mo" plan on Hostinger requires paying 48 months upfront ($119.52 at checkout), whereas SiteGround's "$3.99/mo" intro offer lasts only 12 months ($47.88) before renewing at $17.99/mo. Over a full 36-month website lifecycle, SiteGround's entry plan totals roughly $479.64 in hosting alone, while Cloudways' flat $11.00/mo pay-as-you-go cloud server totals $396.00 with zero contract lock-in.

02. Separating Real Promo Codes from Auto-Applied Offers

Many coupon aggregators publish fabricated strings like "SAVE90NOW" that fail at checkout. SkyeFlare explicitly distinguishes between providers that accept manual promo codes (such as Namecheap's monthly coupon hub) and providers that embed their deepest discounts directly into official checkout URLs without requiring a code field.

03. Hidden Domain Renewal Multipliers

"Free domain included" always applies strictly to Year 1 registration on select TLDs. Beginning in Month 13, registrars and hosts renew `.com` domains at standard rates between $14.95 and $21.99 per year. Our calculator factors domain renewals into every 24- and 36-month projection so your budget stays realistic.
